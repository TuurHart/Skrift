Picked for you. Overrule any of these.

  1. 1Destination keeps its built place: its own row, 12 pt under the pill (as in the app today). It costs the phone header 36 pt. The pill’s row is taken by the fading line.
  2. 2Drag is positional. The pill is cut in four zones, Not rated on the left to Important on the right; where you let go is the tier. In B a plain tap only shows a hint.
  3. 3In A, one tap on Important un-rates the note. A stray tap drops it from the queue and every export (C88). Only the toast says so.
A · tap to step. Each tap on the pill moves one step: Not rated → Passing → Useful → Important → Not rated. No picker opens.
B · drag. Press the pill and slide left or right; the pill shows the tier under your finger. Let go to set it. Arrow keys work too.

The two notes are separate notes with their own rating and destination; each is shared across Phone, iPad and Mac the way sync would. The voice note starts Not rated and Personal; the typed note starts Useful and Idea. Heights are measured live from the rendered frame, top of the title row to the first line of the body; Q50 measured today’s phone voice header at 243 pt and Q70’s pill version at 136 pt without a destination row. The destination row is drawn from Shared/UI/DestinationRowView.swift (one chip at rest; tap for PRIVATE | PORTFOLIO and four segments; a portfolio pick shows its folder in amber) and NoteDestination; in the app it is hidden until the Destinations switch in Settings is on, which the checkbox above mimics. The typed note carries place and weather because typed notes will record them (D151). Other sizes and copy come from MemoPageView.noteHeaderCore, NoteProperties, ThreeBallScale, TagEditorRow, MemoSpine.oneLiner and Palette.swift. The phone nav bar, iPad side panels and Mac window chrome are placeholders.
