Q4 · first pass · C242 C98 · D24 · list row from the signed Q22 one-notes-list

You edited Tiles for the bathroom floor on the iPhone on a plane. Before the phone came back online, the Mac edited the same note. Click through what you see when they meet. Notes are fake.

My picks, to overrule
1. Keep both is the big button, first in the list Shapr3D's own support calls "keep both" the safe choice (C242), so it gets the filled button. The spec's order (this device, the other, both) is reversed. The copy keeps the same title plus "(Mac copy)" or "(iPhone copy)", and the same rating, tags and photo.
2. The prompt opens by itself when you open the note, and the note is read-only until you pick "Later" closes it and leaves an amber banner on the note. Editing a note that already has two versions would make a third, so typing waits. The list row keeps the amber "2 versions" pill until you pick.
3. The version you did not keep goes to Recently Deleted, not to a new history on the note It sits in the existing Fading sheet's Recently Deleted section for the usual 14 seen-days, labelled "replaced". Bring back returns it as its own note, which is Keep both after the fact. C242 says "version history"; that surface does not exist yet. Flip to see it on the note instead:
The one questionThe version you don't keep: should it go to Recently Deleted with every other deleted note (one trash, Bring back makes it its own note), or stay on the note itself as "Other version" for 14 days, which is a new thing on the note screen?
Rules this mock assumes
  • Only words conflict: body, title and tags. Rating, lock, reminder and destination keep today's newest-wins, with no prompt.
  • New notes never conflict (C98). Two devices creating two notes is just two notes.
  • Picking on one device settles it on both. The other device's pill goes at the next sync.
  • The Mac does not polish or export a note with two versions until you pick. Its pill replaces Enhancing.
  • Device names are generic: "this iPhone", "the Mac". Skrift does not store a device's name today.
