Why it's annoying. The field auto-focuses on open (.onAppear { inputFocused = true }), so at the .medium detent the keyboard takes most of the sheet before you've decided anything — the suggestions you'd rather tap are pushed under it. Typing is the primary affordance and picking is the afterthought. And suggestions come only from tags already on your own notes — the phone never reads your vault. The Mac does (VaultTagScanner), the phone has no equivalent.

The photograph-that-gave-you-an-idea is one note, filed Idea. The other person's thing rides along inside it as the picture. Authorship still decides: the idea is yours. Inspiration is the narrower bucket — you liked it, no idea attached yet.

My call

B. Destination belongs on the note next to the rating, not inside a sheet named after something it isn't. The two decisions are the same kind of decision — importance says whether a note may leave, destination says where — and both are one tap with nothing to open. Take version A's tag sheet as B's tag sheet.

C is the one to reject, and for the reason the whole feature exists: it puts "add the word inspiration" and "move this note into the AI-readable repo" one keystroke apart in the same list. A privacy boundary shouldn't be a fuzzy-match away.

Collapsed is the version to build — B1/B2/B3 above. Resting shows one chip, tapping opens all four in place. Personal stays quiet; a portfolio destination names its folder and says it will be read, because that is the only moment the warning means anything. B4 (inline with the tags) saves one line and blurs the one distinction the feature exists to draw — take it only if B1's extra line annoys you in use.
