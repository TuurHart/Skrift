The Web We Lost

By Anil Dash · December 13, 2012

There was a time when the pages you made were yours in a plain and boring way: a folder of files on a server you paid for, with an address you could keep for twenty years.

People linked to each other by hand. A link was a small act of trust, and the web was the sum of millions of those small acts, made by people who mostly did it for free.

What changed

Somewhere along the way the pages moved into a handful of big rooms. The rooms were comfortable and fast, and it took a while to notice that you could not take your things with you when you left.

None of this was a plot. Each step was reasonable on its own, and each one made the next step easier. That is exactly why it is worth writing down while people still remember the other way.

The good news is that the old tools still work. A page you write today, on an address you own, will be readable long after the rooms have changed their rules again.
