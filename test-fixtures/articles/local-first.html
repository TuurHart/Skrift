Local-first software

Martin Kleppmann, Adam Wiggins, Peter van Hardenberg, Mark McGranaghan

April 2019

Why the cloud feels fragile

Most apps keep your work on someone else’s server and hand you a view of it. When the server goes away, so does the work, however carefully you made it. That trade made sense when phones were slow and storage was dear.

Neither is true any more. Your data should live on your own device first, and the network should only carry copies. Then the app works on a plane, in a tunnel, or ten years after the company folds.

What it asks of the software

Two devices will edit the same thing while apart, so the app has to merge without asking you to pick a winner. That is the hard part, and most of this piece is about it. The rest is ordinary craft: fast saves, plain files, and an export you can read without the app.

None of this is free. Sync becomes the product’s spine, and every new feature has to say how it merges. We think the cost is worth it for anything a person means to keep.
