> Why iron makes blue glazes go green
> 
> Most studio potters meet this the same way: a cobalt blue that looks perfect on a white body turns olive at the edges on a darker one. The culprit is usually not the glaze at all but iron oxide in the clay body migrating into the glaze layer during the melt.
> 
> At cone 6 the migration is stronger than at cone 5, which is why the same glaze can look right at the lower temperature and wrong at the higher one. Two fixes exist: switch to a lower-iron body, or add a slip layer that seals the body under the glaze.
> 
> Neither is free. A new body means re-testing every glaze you own, and a slip adds a step to every piece. But it is the difference between a blue and a green.
